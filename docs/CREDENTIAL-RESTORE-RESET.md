# Secure restore reset

The first reset slice is the offline OAuth continuation-denial phase. It does
not expose an operator command, authenticate an approval, stop an incumbent or
admit a new writer. The accepting composition must retain the genuine approval,
authenticated executor, actual incumbent exclusion and unpublished creator FD.
The complete credential, session, authority, delegation and budget reset joins
this phase in one owning transaction; this slice alone is not complete restore.

The immutable cut commits to the selected external authority, exact credential
namespace, source and target incarnation/epoch, complete archive digest, current
custody binding and full bounded OAuth row/schema inventory. Reference schema
comes from the actual canonical installers. An imported partial, old, unknown,
shadow or attached schema refuses; it is never repaired by reset. Captured rows
and platform scope/schema are compared again before mutation. The initial
kernel's supported source epoch is one and target epoch is two.

In one savepoint, reset records the cut and closes every captured single-use
Connect, Refresh, management, Use, Fresh-owner and inbound-grant identity. Use
claims also have a stable invocation cut, so copying or retiming an old handle
under a new reference does not bypass it. All connection slots require new authorization
and advance their checked local epoch. Active inbound grants/refresh are
revoked; allocated or offered Fresh originals are refused. Consumed originals
remain historical records. These local epochs are independent of the external
security epoch and are not compared numerically to it.

Connect/refresh uncertainty, original times, receipts, encrypted tokens,
verifiers, codes, quarantine and existing material versions are retained.
No provider revocation is sent and no May/unknown outcome becomes "not sent" or
a fabricated rejection. Real callback, load, dispatch, publication and cached
completion paths consult the current closure. A matching old row or historical
receipt cannot reopen it. New identifiers still require every existing current
authentication, purpose, account, binding and generation check. In particular,
there is no implicit adoption of a restored connection slot; the existing
replacement-profile refusal stays in place.

Immutability refers to the cut and its closed-identity ledger. Ordinary bounded
reclamation of expired, unsent management navigation and read-only Use rows may
still run; it cannot remove that independent closure or create authority.
Cut-closed callback, cancellation, expiry and definitive-rejection paths refuse
before custody work or rewriting old uncertainty. May-to-uncertain bookkeeping
may still truthfully refine an unknown outcome; it cannot issue or reopen a permit.
An explicit future fresh-reauthorization profile must also keep old generations'
history and custody separate from later disconnect/cleanup. This slice retains
the existing dispatched-slot and replacement refusals rather than claiming that
new profile is ready.

The ordinary restore preflight independently refuses occupied OAuth or
Fresh-owner history even when current declarations have been removed and even
before any cut exists. Its bounded read-only scan preserves the caller's
progress handler and never installs a schema on the imported connection. Empty
current metadata remains allowed; unknown or partial shapes refuse.

A database cut cannot retract plaintext already held by App/Shell or settle an
already-armed send. Genuine incumbent drain remains mandatory before reset and
same-created-file writer admission. Unsupported callback or companion profiles
continue refusing. The callback-reservation model's fixture tables are not a
production schema and are never invented here. Budget usage, holds and uncertain
debt must be preserved and frozen by the complete reset composition. Existing
ciphertext is not relabelled under a new epoch or moved to a different vault.

Portable tests exercise actual SQLite/crypto and real consumer paths, including
copy/rewind, unknown outcomes, read-only preflight and progress-hook survival.
Process-loss cuts distinguish savepoint release from the owning commit and
reopen twice against independent expected state. They qualify process-loss
atomicity, not host power loss, native physical exclusion or live-provider work.
